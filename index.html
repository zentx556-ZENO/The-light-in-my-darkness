THE LIGHT IN MY DARKNESS

A story about a heart that had forgotten how to hope.

02 — THE DREAM

There was a woman who visited me in my dreams.

She never spoke. She never turned around. She simply stood at a distance, dressed in a white dress and a white shirt, her figure illuminated by a light I could never reach. No matter how far I walked toward her, the distance between us remained the same. And no matter how desperately I tried, I could never see her face.

Yet, every night, I found myself searching for her.

I didn't understand why. How could I miss someone whose face I had never seen? How could I fall in love with someone who had never spoken a single word to me? It made no sense, not even to myself. But there was something about her presence that made the loneliness in my chest feel less unbearable. For a little while, the world stopped hurting.

Then I would wake up.

And the darkness would be waiting.

THE DARKEST DAYS

My life had become a routine of pretending. I laughed when I was expected to laugh, answered when someone spoke to me, and smiled whenever silence threatened to reveal too much. I had become so good at convincing people that I was fine that even I began to wonder whether there was anything left of me beneath the act.

Some days, getting out of bed felt like a battle no one would ever know I had fought. Other days, I moved through the hours like a ghost, watching people live their lives while I struggled to find a reason to feel present in my own.

I didn't need the world to understand me. I had stopped expecting that a long time ago.

I only wished that, for once, something would give me a reason to look forward to tomorrow.

03 — THE ENCOUNTER

Then I met her.

It was an ordinary day, the kind I would have forgotten without a second thought. I was walking through a hallway when I noticed a girl standing near the window. Sunlight spilled across the floor beside her, catching the edges of her hair as she spoke to someone nearby.

I don't know what made me stop.

Perhaps it was the way she laughed, completely unaware of how beautiful an ordinary moment could be. Perhaps it was the warmth in her voice, or the way her eyes seemed to brighten when she listened to someone talk. Or perhaps it was simply the fact that, for the first time in a long while, I found myself wanting to stay somewhere instead of wishing I could disappear.

There was something familiar about her, though I couldn't explain it. A strange ache stirred in my chest, like remembering a place I had never been.

For a fleeting moment, I wondered if I had finally found the person I had been searching for in my sleep.

But I dismissed the thought. Dreams were dreams, and reality had never been kind enough to resemble them.

I didn't approach her that day. I didn't even know her name.

Still, I remembered her.

And the next time I saw her, I remembered her again.

04 — THE LIGHT

Soon, I began noticing things I would once have overlooked. The way she tilted her head when she was confused. How she sometimes laughed so hard that she had to catch her breath. The little expressions that crossed her face when she thought no one was paying attention. The way she could turn the most insignificant story into something worth listening to.

I began to understand that happiness didn't always arrive as a grand revelation. Sometimes, it came in the form of an unexpected conversation. Sometimes, it was a shared joke on an otherwise miserable afternoon. Sometimes, it was simply seeing someone you cared about walk through the door and realizing that the day had become a little easier to bear.

I started looking forward to things again.

Not because my problems had disappeared, nor because my life had suddenly become perfect, but because there was now something within my days that I genuinely wanted to experience.

Her.

And the more I came to appreciate her, the more I realized how little she knew about the difference she made.

She didn't know that I sometimes replayed our conversations in my head when the nights grew too quiet. She didn't know that her smallest acts of kindness stayed with me long after she had forgotten them. She didn't know that there were mornings when I felt exhausted before the day had even begun, and the thought of seeing her was enough to convince me to get up.

She probably thought she was doing nothing special.

That was the beautiful thing about her.

She never had to try to save me. She never had to find the right words or understand every wound I carried. She didn't need to know the battles I fought in silence or the reasons behind the sadness I rarely showed. She simply treated me like someone worth talking to, someone worth knowing, someone who deserved to be there.

And perhaps that was what I had been missing all along.

WHAT LOVE MEANS

I had spent so much of my life believing that I needed to become someone better before I could deserve happiness. I thought I had to fix every broken part of myself, bury every painful memory, and learn how to stand alone before I could let another person into my life.

But she never asked me to be someone else.

Around her, I didn't feel the need to pretend that I had everything figured out. I could be quiet without feeling invisible. I could laugh without wondering how long the happiness would last. I could have a bad day without believing that one bad day defined the rest of my life.

She didn't make me forget my darkness.

She made me realize that my darkness wasn't the only thing left of me.

Sometimes, I wonder whether she understands how much I appreciate her. Whether she knows that there is a difference between someone who makes you happy and someone who reminds you that happiness is still possible. Whether she realizes that, in a world where I had grown tired of expecting anything good, she became something I never wanted to take for granted.

I wish I could tell her.

I wish I could explain that I appreciate her not because she is perfect, but because she is human. Because she has days when she is tired, moments when she doubts herself, and feelings she might not always know how to express. Because she can be happy and sad, confident and uncertain, strong and vulnerable, all without becoming any less wonderful to me.

I don't want to love only the parts of her that make my life brighter.

I want to appreciate the person she is, even on the days when she doesn't feel bright at all.

Because she deserves to be loved on her difficult days, too. She deserves someone who notices when her smile is a little less genuine, who listens when her words come slowly, and who stays when she no longer has the energy to make everything seem okay.

If she has ever felt the same loneliness I once knew, I hope I can give her even a fraction of the comfort she unknowingly gave me.

I hope that when the world becomes too heavy for her, she will have somewhere to rest.

I hope I can become someone whose presence makes her feel safe, someone who listens without judgment and cares without keeping score. I hope I can remind her that she doesn't have to be extraordinary to be cherished, just as she reminded me that I didn't have to be whole to be worthy of love.

Perhaps that is what love truly means.

Not finding someone who rescues you from every terrible thing that happens, but finding someone whose existence makes you want to keep living, growing, and becoming a little kinder to yourself. And then, in return, doing your best to make their world gentler, too.

THE FINAL REFLECTION

I still have difficult days. The darkness hasn't vanished, and I know it may never disappear completely. There are nights when old thoughts return, when the weight of everything feels familiar again, when I wonder if I have really changed at all.

But now, I know that a difficult day is only a day. It isn't my entire life.

I have learned to appreciate the sunlight through my window, the sound of laughter in a crowded hallway, and the quiet comfort of knowing that somewhere in this enormous world, there is a person whose existence has become precious to me.

And I have learned that I don't need to wait until my life is perfect to be grateful for it.

Because I have already been given something beautiful.

Not a promise that I will never hurt again. Not a guarantee that everything will work out the way I hope. Just the chance to have met someone who made me feel, even for a moment, that life was worth looking at a little more closely.

I don't know what the future holds for us. I don't know if she will stay in my life forever, if our paths will eventually separate, or if there will come a day when I have to learn how to carry these feelings without seeing her anymore.

The thought frightens me.

But I refuse to let the fear of losing something beautiful prevent me from appreciating that it exists.

If our time together is brief, I will still be grateful. If she never learns how much she has changed me, I will still be grateful. And if she never comes to love me the way I have come to love her, I hope I will still have the courage to wish her happiness, even if that happiness takes her somewhere I cannot follow.

Because she was never obligated to save me.

Her kindness was never a debt she owed me. Her smile was never a promise. She was simply living her life, being herself, unaware that somewhere along the way, a boy who had nearly forgotten how to hope had found something worth hoping for.

And that alone is enough to make me thankful.

Sometimes, I think back to the person I used to be. The boy who wandered through his days without looking forward to anything. The boy who believed that the world had already shown him everything it had to offer, and that whatever remained would only be more of the same.

I wish I could tell him that he was wrong.

That one day, he would meet someone who would make him notice the beauty he had spent so long ignoring. Someone who would remind him that there were still conversations to have, memories to make, and mornings worth waking up for.

Someone who would teach him that even a heart accustomed to darkness could still learn to love the light.

And if I could go back and tell that boy just one thing, I wouldn't tell him that everything would be okay. I wouldn't promise him that his suffering would end or that the future would be kind.

I would simply tell him to keep going.

Because somewhere ahead of him, a girl was living her life, completely unaware that she would one day become one of the reasons he was grateful to be alive.

And perhaps, someday, I will finally gather the courage to tell her everything I have kept to myself: how her laughter stayed with me, how her kindness mattered, how her ordinary presence became extraordinary in my eyes. How, without ever asking to be important, she became someone I will always be thankful to have known.

Until then, I will appreciate her in the ways I can. I will listen when she speaks, cherish the moments we share, and never allow the familiarity of her presence to make me forget how fortunate I am to have it.

I may never know whether she was the person I had been searching for all those nights, or whether my dreams were simply my lonely heart trying to imagine a reason to keep going.

But I know who she is to me now.

And as I look back on the life I once thought was nothing but an endless stretch of darkness, I finally understand what I could never put into words.
